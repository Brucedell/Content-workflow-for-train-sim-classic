The opportunity analysis recommends Shopify or WooCommerce with Stripe/PayPal and a delivery plugin; that is route B/C. For a first launch with a handful of products, route A gets the same customer experience (link, library, updates) with far less to run, and the landing site makes it look bespoke anyway. Moving from A to B later is a product-by-product re-upload, not a rebuild.

5 · The customer's “Personal Depot”

The analysis calls for an account area where a buyer sees lifetime re-downloads and which products just received a framework patch. Every provider in route A already gives buyers a library page with their purchases and versions; you get the “depot” by:

  • naming it so on your site (a “My depot” link in the menu that points at the provider's library login), and
  • using the provider's update feature when you re-upload a product: buyers get the email, the library shows the new version.

What you do not get on route A is a cross-product dashboard of your own design (“three of your locos use framework v2.3”). Keep that as a page on the landing site: a Compatibility board listing each product, its current version and the framework version it needs. Static text, updated by you with each release.

6 · Where the 3D viewer sits

  • Landing page hero: a random vehicle from the pool, full-width, with the hotspot buttons. This is the “proof of mastery” moment. demo
  • Product pages on your site: the same viewer with that product's model only; buttons for its signature features (“Line breaker”, “Trolley pole”, “Cab doors”).
  • Provider's product page: usually images and video only. Some providers allow an embedded iframe or custom HTML check; otherwise link back to your product page (“Interactive preview ↗”).
  • Dev log posts: drop the viewer with a work-in-progress model to show progress on a crowdfunded project.
