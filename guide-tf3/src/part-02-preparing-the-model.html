1 · Three habits that make the model dual-target

  1. Every moving or physically meaningful part is its own object with its origin on its axis. TSC merges static parts anyway; TF3 needs axles as separate nodes (“the origin should be aligned in the center in terms of the x and z axis. Otherwise the axle will wobble ingame”). manual Wheels modelled as one mesh per bogie look fine in TSC and are unusable in TF3.
  2. Keep the TSC naming as master, and never rely on Blender suffixes. The TF3 importer strips #…, and the add-on turns 1_0500_bo01wh02 into w2; a stray .001 would become part of a TSC node name and a TF3 mesh file name.
  3. Place a driver empty (tsc_driver) and keep the pantograph, doors and wipers as independent actions. TSC uses the empty for DriverPosition; TF3 turns it into a crew seat locator. Independent actions become TSC .ia files and TF3 event strips without re-keying.

2 · Axes and the rotation at the branch

  • Export FBX with Forward = X, Up = Z and tick Use Blender Coordinate System in the Model Editor's Im-/Export options. manual
  • Scale: metres in both games. Apply scale before export (TSC rule; FBX exports with apply_scale_options = FBX_SCALE_ALL).
  • Origin: the vehicle centre on the rail head in both games. check that TF3's ground plane (“gray ground face at height 0” in the Model Editor) is the rail head for rail vehicles, not the sleeper top.
  • Axle rotation axis: TF3 spins axles about their local y axis. After the −90° rotation, the TSC wheel objects (which spin about Blender X) rotate about TF3 y. The add-on rotates whole root objects, so children keep their relative orientation.

Why not name things the TF3 way from the start?

Because TSC's keyword names (bo01, bo01wh01, panto01, wiper01) are what BRIAGE uses to keep parts un-merged and what the TSC bogie blueprint references. TF3 has no reserved names at all, so it is the flexible side. Keep the strict naming as master and generate the lax one.

4 · LODs: from name prefixes to files

  • Visible distances are set per LOD in the Model Editor (visibleFrom/visibleTo), not in names. manual Your TSC distances (500, 1200) are a good starting point.
  • A node that exists in LOD 0 but not in LOD 1 simply disappears at distance. Animated parts (doors, pantograph) usually need at least a crude LOD 1 version; in TSC they often have none.
  • Fake bogies must be listed once per LOD (fakeBogies = { {…}, {…} }) even if empty. manual

6 · Materials and the shortcode tag

In the FBX, a material name is <materialname>[|<shortcode>][#<comment>]. The shortcode selects the TF3 material type; without it the importer predicts a type (or your .import.lua's predictMaterial does). manual Names starting with / reference existing materials (e.g. ::/vehicle/train/emissive/train_all_lights.mtl for lights). manual

9 · What to leave out

  • Cab interior and pick meshes: TF3 has no cab view. Keep them in a separate TSC scene or tag tf3_skip.
  • Stencil shadow meshes and the collision IGS hull: TF3 computes shadows and uses |collider_list instead.
  • Number digit planes: use labels.
  • Coupler empties: TF3 has no couplers to author; vehicle spacing comes from the bounding box / extent check.
  • Light glow meshes can stay if you want visible lamps, re-materialled to the base-game emissive material and switched by the …_parts_on/off events.
