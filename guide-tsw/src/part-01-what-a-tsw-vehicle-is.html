What a Train Sim World vehicle actually is

In TSC a vehicle is a blueprint graph; in TSW it is a small family of Unreal assets that split what the vehicle is from what it looks like. This part reads that family the way the game does, follows one key press to a turning wheel, and maps every TSC idea you already have onto its TSW counterpart.

1 · Model and view: why the split exists

The RVM (model) is created when the route loads and is never destroyed, even for trains parked off-map in the “vault”. The RVV (view) is spawned when the player comes near and destroyed when they go far. The RVA (audio) comes and goes with the view. That one design decision explains most of what follows:

  • Anything that must remember state (throttle position, doors, lights, fuel) lives on the RVM, tagged SaveGame.
  • The RVV can be heavy (the Class 47's has 221 components) because only a handful exist at once.
  • Keyboard input goes to the RVM, not the RVV, so the AI driver and the HUD work when the visuals are gone.
  • A control (VHID) is always two components: a logic half on the RVM and a clickable half on the RVV, linked by name.

Spawning needs a formation

Nothing spawns an RVD directly. A TrainFormation data asset (FRM_) lists RVDs in order; timetables, scenarios and the spawn menu pick formations. Make FRM_MyLoco containing just RVD_MyLoco so the loco can be placed on its own.

A wagon is a locomotive with the driving parts removed: the 12-ton van's RVM has the four inherited components, a Simulation, two couplers, two vacuum hoses, four door components, push buttons for handbrake and door locks, and a NumberingComponent. No throttle, no environments, no seats.

The RVM component list is the feature list

Adding a feature means adding a model component here, a matching hitbox on the RVV, and usually a few nodes in the event graph or the SimuGraph. That is the same shape as TSC's “control value + animation + Lua handler”, just in three places you can see.

This is where your Blender work lands: the two skeletal meshes, their animation blueprints, the collision mesh and the sockets that everything else attaches to. Parts 2 and 3 prepare them; Part 4 wires them in.

6 · The cooked-asset rule

Everything that ships with the game is cooked: editor data stripped. You can place, spawn and read Dovetail's vehicles at runtime, but you cannot open their Blueprint graphs or SimuGraphs, and Dovetail's FAQ confirms you cannot modify existing trains. Everything in this set about Dovetail's vehicles was read from the running game through Python (Part 6 §6), not by opening the assets. Your own plugin is uncooked source, so none of those limits apply to it. verified
