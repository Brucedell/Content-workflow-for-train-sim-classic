What a TSC vehicle actually is

The game never sees your Blender model. It sees a bundle of blueprints that point at geometry, animations, curves and scripts, tied together by named nodes and named control values. Understand that bundle and every rule in the later parts makes sense.

1 · A vehicle is a graph of files, not a model

When you place a locomotive in a scenario, TSC loads one vehicle blueprint (an engine, wagon or tender blueprint). That file contains almost no geometry. Instead it references other files by path, and those files reference more files. The figure below is the real graph of the Kuju sample electric locomotive, with its actual file names. verified

Key idea

Your Blender scene produces only the orange boxes. Everything blue, green and purple is authored in BPE2, a text editor or a Lua editor. The game joins them by names: file paths, node names, animation IDs and control names. Most “it doesn't work” problems are a broken name somewhere in this graph.

3 · How the game places a vehicle on the track

TSC treats the body as a rigid object carried by its bogie pivots. Bogies follow the track spline and turn about their pivot points. Wheels are just nodes that spin at a rate derived from speed and wheel radius. Couplers are points at each end that lock to the neighbouring vehicle's receiving point. The drawing below uses the sample electric's real numbers.

4 · Three coordinate systems and how they map

The third system: blueprint 2D fields

Many vehicle blueprint fields describe a side view and use only two axes:

BlueprintTSC fileBlender
X (along track)ZY
Y (height)YZ

So BogeyPivotX = 6.25 means “6.25 m forward of the origin”, which is Blender Y = 6.25.

Matrices (FrontCouplingPivot, DriverPosition) are full 4×4 transforms in TSC file axes, row-major, with the translation in elements 13–15. Example from the sample: … 0, 1.025, 9.025, 1 = 1.025 m up, 9.025 m forward. verified

5 · Control values: the bus everything talks through

A control value is a named floating-point number on a vehicle, declared in the vehicle blueprint with a default, minimum and maximum. It is the single most important concept for custom behaviour: every part of a vehicle communicates only by reading and writing control values.

Rules of the bus

  • Names are the contract. The simulation finds its inputs by name. The brake is explicit: the simulation's CabControlName must equal a ControlName in the vehicle blueprint (TrainBrakeControl in both samples) verified. Throttle and reverser use the conventional names Regulator and Reverser; keep them check.
  • Range maps to animation. A cab lever's .ia is played scrubbed to the value: minimum at the first frame, maximum at the last. Model and key every lever from its minimum position to its maximum.
  • Defaults are spawn state. DefaultValue is the value at spawn. The sample electric's TrainBrakeControl default of 0.7 means it spawns braked; the steam example's 1.0 means fully applied. verified
  • Custom controls are free. A control value does not need a lever. Declare TrolleyPoleFront with range 0–1, point an exterior animation at it, and let Lua write it. This is the backbone of every scripted feature in Part 5.
  • Difficulty filters elements. Interface elements carry a DifficultyType (StopGo / Intermediate / Expert), which is how the sample has both SimpleThrottle and Regulator on the same physical lever.

6 · The traction and braking chain

You do not program physics directly; you give the subsystem limits and lookup curves. This is the working model to design against. The “minimum of three limits” rule comes from the project's handoff document and is consistent with both samples. check Verify final behaviour in game with Lua (Call("GetTractiveEffort")) rather than trusting the model.

Design rules that follow
  • MaxForce must be at least the peak of the throttle and speed curves, or the top of the curves is unreachable. The sample electric does this exactly (throttle curve 0→300, MaxForce 300). verified
  • The throttle curve must start at 0,0 or the vehicle creeps at zero throttle.
  • End the speed curve at or beyond MaxSpeed.
  • Mass and friction decide whether that force can be used at all.
Unit labels are not conversions

BPE2 stores a mUnits label next to what you typed, but the samples show the stored value equals the typed value (the 7F's MaxForce is typed as 35.296 “Lb/f”, stored as 35.296; the real engine's tractive effort is 35,296 lbf). Treat labels as hints, compare with a known-good vehicle, and measure in game. verified
